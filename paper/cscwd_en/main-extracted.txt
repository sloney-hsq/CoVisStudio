CoVisStudio: Separating Evidence from Edit Scope
in Full-Duplex Collaborative Visual Analysis
Anonymous review draft
Abstract—When people revise analytical dashboards with an
AI assistant, reusing previously discussed objects need not mean
restoring their historical filters. Full-duplex interaction allows
revisions during explanation and execution, while generated
speech, delivered speech, and the visible canvas can differ.
We present CoVisStudio, which represents a revision through
object provenance, current targets and changes, and retained
or released conditions. A correctable revision card exposes this
interpretation over execution capabilities shared with a strong
direct-editing baseline. The system separately records commits,
rendering, visibility, and output-sample playback. A local browser
workflow passed 24 mechanism checks. An earlier eight-run real-
model diagnostic produced four correct final states, whereas its
original driver marked three passes. Two subsequent focused
real-model checks separately exposed a repeated integer-string
rejection and a successful historical-member transfer with 14.8
seconds of naturally completed output. Inspection also found
an outdated image in a post-commit follow-up; its repair has
only local verification. We specify a prospective two-condition,
full-duplex study of correctness, understanding, and repair cost.
The evidence supports implementation feasibility and identifies
reliability gaps; it does not establish user benefit or full-duplex
superiority.
Index Terms—human–AI collaboration, full-duplex speech,
visual analytics, intent revision, shared evidence
I. INTRODUCTION
Creating a dashboard is rarely a single translation of a
complete specification. People inspect charts, select com-
parison objects, change measures, and narrow the scope as
their questions develop. With an AI assistant, collaborators
must continually establish what they are discussing, what has
changed, and what remains valid. This is coordination around
a shared analytical artifact, beyond converting language into
chart commands.
Consider three charts showing order volume, low-review
rate, and delivery time. A user first examines three priority
categories in the Brazilian state of RJ, then moves to MG.
During the assistant’s explanation, the user asks: “Use those
three categories you just discussed for the two service charts;
keep the current region and dates.” Correct execution must
recover the historical members, target the specified current
views, and retain MG. Restoring the earlier query or selecting
MG’s current top three may yield numerically correct charts
that answer a different question.
Full-duplex speech provides an entry point for timely re-
vision, but does not ensure shared understanding. Generated
words may not have played; a committed chart may not have
rendered; a reference to an explanation may concern an earlier
set rather than the latest one. The final chart and a complete
chat transcript alone cannot establish the evidence available
when a revision arose.
Prior work supports ambiguity management, speech–canvas
collaboration, and intervention during execution [1]–[3]. Nei-
ther interruption nor combining speech with images is our
contribution. We ask: In collaboration that permits revision
during delivery, can explicitly separating referenced evidence
from the current edit scope help collaborators execute and un-
derstand changes, and at what interaction cost? Both planned
conditions use full-duplex interaction; we do not compare it
with sequential interaction.
We contribute an implemented revision representation sep-
arating historical object identity from current filters, measures,
and targets, with a correctable shared card. We also connect
inputs, execution, and delivery in a verification workflow,
report technical successes and preserved failures, and specify
a comparison that retains the baseline’s exact-member reuse
capabilities. User benefit remains the hypothesis this protocol
will test.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Natural-Language Analysis and Ambiguity
DataTone exposes ambiguity and partial query constraints,
letting users correct interpretations [1]. Our emphasis is a
particular revision ambiguity: historical members can remain
relevant while their old calculation scope should not return. We
separate object provenance from application scope and record
relevant delivered speech and visible versions. This is a change
of focus, not evidence that a newer speech interface surpasses
ambiguity widgets.
Speech-to-Visualization investigates generating visualiza-
tion queries from spoken questions and database informa-
tion [4]. ChartEditBench evaluates grounded, multi-turn edit-
ing of existing charts and code [5]. These provide direct
foundations for speech-driven creation and chart editing. We
examine whether historical-evidence reuse preserves the re-
quested objects and current conditions, rather than adding chart
types.
B. Speech and Shared-Canvas Collaboration
CommSketch combines sketching and narration to commu-
nicate design intent [6]. CLEO tracks user actions in a shared
design canvas and selectively revises plans during concurrent
human–agent work [2]. VoCa studies speech–canvas coordina-
tion, feedback, and user control [3]. Shared visual work and
mid-execution revision therefore have clear precedents.

Analytical dashboards offer independently checkable mem-
bers, filters, measures, and untouched views. This makes it
possible to examine the case where historical objects remain
referable without restoring their scope. That setting alone is
insuﬀicient novelty: a fair comparison must determine whether
explicit support improves revision and understanding.
C. Full-Duplex Execution and Common Ground
Full-Duplex-Bench-v3 evaluates tool use under realistic
disfluency [7]; DuplexAgent-RSI addresses coordination of
realtime speech with background execution, including delivery
of invalidated results [8]. We use interruption and commit
management as engineering foundations rather than claiming
a new coordination algorithm. Grounding requires collabo-
rators to provide and check evidence of understanding [9].
Accordingly, a correct internal member selection and a user’s
knowledge of that selection are separate outcomes.
III. REVISION REPRESENTATION AND METHOD
A. Referenced Evidence and Current State
A revision episode comprises a user utterance and subse-
quent clarification, execution, and repair. It need not equal
one provider response: a request can generate tools, follow-up
speech, and several delivery events. Let Ci denote the current
query configuration of view i, including region, dates, order
status, measure, and membership rule. Historical evidence
e contains its source version and member set M(e). We
represent a revision as
R = (O, T, ∆, P, L),
(1)
where O is object provenance, T the current target views, ∆
the explicit changes, P retained conditions, and L explicitly
released conditions. The interface organizes these into three
parts: object evidence; targets and changes; retained and
released conditions. They derive from expressed requests and
available evidence, not evaluator answers.
In the opening example, M(e) supplies the categories,
views 2 and 3 are targets, and MG, current dates, delivered
status, and each measure are retained. View 1 is unchanged.
“Release those categories and find MG’s top three again”
instead removes fixed membership and establishes an explicit
ranking. Keeping a fixed set differs from preserving a dynamic
ranking rule.
B. Execution Rules and Correctable Support
The system lists existing historical charts and saved sets,
from which the model can select a candidate identifier. Users
can also select evidence directly. Both paths share member
resolution; historical reuse copies object identity, not the old
region, dates, or measure. For target views,

An explicit request can compute and commit through one
flat-argument revise_analysis call: targets, membership
action, candidate identifier, and changed query fields. B0 and
B2 share the compiler, candidate tools, and revision structure.
B2 exposes a summary and expandable card; B0 retains direct
execution feedback. Thus the treatment is a package of visible,
correctable revision support, not a different binding algorithm
or exclusive access to exact members.
Cards do not impose confirmation on every operation.
An unresolved choice pauses publication for clarification; a
clear request can apply immediately, leaving its card available
for inspection and correction through a new request. Such
a card may therefore appear after commitment. Generation,
viewport exposure, opening, and successful user confirmation
are separately recorded. None alone proves agreement or
understanding.
C. The Role of Full-Duplex Interaction
Revision can arrive while an explanation is partially deliv-
ered, a tool remains unfinished, or a chart has just changed
(Fig. 1). The challenge includes semantic reference errors
even when the selected operation executes correctly, beyond
preventing stale results from overwriting newer ones.
On a speech-start event, the system revokes old work’s
future publication permission and stops old playback before
requesting remote cancellation. Earlier lawful commits remain.
Guarantees begin with local event handling, not physical
speech onset; delivered audio cannot be recalled. An explicitly
named RJ set may be resolved without playback position,
whereas “the group you just mentioned” may depend on
delivered explanation. Human coding must establish that de-
pendence. Different candidate sets yielding different charts
does not itself show that full-duplex evidence is necessary.
IV. SYSTEM AND EVIDENCE RECORDS
A. Interface, Data, and Tools
CoVisStudio uses an English Vue/ECharts three-chart inter-
face and a FastAPI backend managing sessions, tools, and pub-
lic chart state. Voice uses Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash-Realtime [10]
and connects only after an explicit start. Charts, conversation,
saved sets, historical inspection, and revision cards form the
workspace. The card displays actual changed and retained
condition values, rather than field names alone.
Queries use real Olist data [11]. Order volume, low-review
rate, and delivery time retain their implemented definitions.
Low-review rate uses valid reviewed orders as the denomina-
tor; delivery time uses eligible delivered records. An order can
contribute to several category groups, so category counts are
not simply summed as a unique-order total.
Tools compute private candidates. Publication jointly checks
current request permission and matching chart version. Both
conditions share reads, saved-set tools, data computation, and
commit rules. Spoken success cannot publish state. Request-
scoped receipts distinguish committed, displayed, and no chart
changes; an earlier success cannot certify a later request.

AudioWorklet source cursors distinguish received, output,

C. An Earlier Eight-Run Real-Model Diagnostic
Four authored request families were each run once through
direct and bound_revision, using real browser/Olist exe-

reference tasks preselected evidence, testing member transfer
and scope retention rather than resolving reference from heard
words.
Four final states satisfied the authored goals, with commits,

The raw driver marked three passes: one correct direct run

later comprehension questions. This is not eight minutes of
continuous voice. Participants need not interrupt at scripted
moments.
B. Independent Intent Coding and Measures
Two coders will independently specify objects, targets,
changes, retention, and release from utterances, explicit se-
lections, and evidence available then. Model proposals and
final outcomes are hidden until intent is locked; necessary
prior explanation and the contemporaneous canvas remain
accessible. Agreement, disagreements, and unknown intent
will be reported. Independent SQL computes reference results
after intent coding; evaluator material never enters model
input.
Primary measures are final revision correctness and under-
standing. Correctness requires the last explicit intent, valid
retained conditions, and independently verified data; an incom-
plete task with known intent may be incorrect. Comprehension
questions address object provenance, affected views, and cal-
culation scope, each scored 0/1 by frozen rules and averaged.
Missing or indeterminate answers remain unknown rather than
inferred from card opening.
Secondary measures include human-confirmed misunder-
standing repairs, repair duration, and total task time. Ex-
ploration, deliberate goal changes, and intermediate incom-
pleteness are not automatically errors. Explanation–commit
consistency is coded separately from playback completion.
Card exposure and confirmation are process observations.
C. Analysis and Decision Scope
All assigned tasks enter the primary analysis, including no-
interruption, failed, timed-out, and unknown-intent tasks. The
first formally begun attempt is primary; repeats remain sep-
arate, without best/latest-attempt substitution. Existing latest-
attempt exports cannot directly implement this rule. Four tasks
from one person are not four independent participants.
We will report known successes, failures, unknowns, and
effect bounds, with 95% intervals from 10,000 participant-
cluster bootstrap samples. With no unknowns, each person’s

mean for the two tasks per condition yields a paired difference;
two-sided paired t tests use Holm correction across the two
primary outcomes at familywise α = 0.05. With unknowns,
complete-case tests are sensitivity descriptions only. Family,
version, and order adjustments use participant fixed effects
with participant-level resampling, without selecting models for
significance.

Timing retains failures and timeouts, reporting correct com-

correctness are censored at termination. Sample size, equal
voice budget, materials, and coding rules will be frozen after
an outcome-blind feasibility assessment.
VII. DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS
Separating object provenance from current edit scope pro-
vides a concrete shared interpretation to inspect and correct.
Its value must be assessed through correctness, understanding,
and cost together. Better understanding alone would support
an understanding claim; improved correctness with longer
task time would imply a tradeoff. Wide uncertainty or a
nonsignificant result would not establish equivalence.
Both conditions permit interruption, so the study cannot
establish superiority over text or sequential speech. Full-
duplex is the setting in which partially delivered explanation
and unfinished execution may coexist. If natural tasks rarely
contain references dependent on delivered speech, conclusions
should concern multi-turn analytical revision rather than a
full-duplex-specific benefit. DataTone’s ambiguity feedback,
CLEO’s concurrent intervention, and VoCa’s speech–canvas
coordination remain direct precedents; a new model, dataset,
or timestamp alone does not establish an advance.
The system currently revokes work on every detected utter-
ance and does not distinguish backchannels. Native history is
not isolated, and sample cursors are not automatically aligned
to words. Real diagnostics are authored, sparse, version-
dependent, and often use preselected evidence; they do not
estimate population success or collaboration benefit. Human
listening, semantic consistency, repair coding, and participant
outcomes remain pending. Visual exposure is not reading,
just as received output is not comprehension. Private records
preserve inputs, versions, receipts, media, and failures; cre-
dentials, raw data, and personal records are not automatically
publishable artifacts.
VIII. CONCLUSION
CoVisStudio separates historical object evidence from cur-
rent analytical scope and exposes a correctable revision struc-
ture over shared tools and compilation. It distinguishes com-
mitment, display, playback, and semantic correctness. Local
execution and real-model diagnostics demonstrate feasible
paths while exposing unexecuted promises, argument rejection,
cancellation errors, and outdated follow-up images. The latest
focused checks separately contain one repeated type failure
and one successful transfer with naturally completed output;
the image-order repair has only local validation. A prospective
strong-baseline comparison will test whether visible support
improves revision and understanding at acceptable repair cost.
User effectiveness and full-duplex superiority are not yet
established.
